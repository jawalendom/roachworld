I have been leaving a recorder running in the kitchen overnight. Not for any
project. I told myself it was to catch the radiator knock and time it properly,
but that was a cover story I invented for the version of me that needs reasons.
The truth is I wanted to know what the room does when I am not in it.

Here is the inventory, from one Tuesday into Wednesday, timestamps approximate.

## 00:14 — the fridge decides something

The compressor kicks in with a sound like a small throat clearing, runs for
eleven minutes, and stops. It does this all night on a cycle that is almost but
not quite regular. If you speed the recording up, the fridge becomes the pulse
of the whole file, a slow tide under everything else.

## 01:40 — pipes

Someone upstairs runs a tap. The building's plumbing carries it down through the
wall as a long descending note, almost musical, and then a knock as the pipe
settles. I have twelve versions of this knock now, from twelve different nights,
and they are all slightly different pitches. I could probably tune the building
if I had a reason to.

> The room is never silent. Silence is just a level of attention you have not
> reached yet.

## 02:55 — the part I keep

This is why I keep doing it. At two fifty-five, most nights, there is a stretch
of about ninety seconds where nothing mechanical happens. The fridge is off. The
pipes are quiet. And what is left is not silence — it is the room's own noise
floor, a soft grey wash that the cheap microphone renders almost like rain. Under
that, if the night is clear, a hum from the tower on the hill. Under *that*, the
downstairs television, still on, playing to no one.

I put headphones on and listen to those ninety seconds more often than I would
admit to a doctor. It is the sound of the household running without supervision.
The Super does his rounds. The fridge breathes. The tower transmits whatever a
tower transmits at three in the morning. And the room holds all of it in a kind
of suspension, waiting for the kettle and the footsteps and the light.

## 05:30 — I come back

The first thing on every recording is me: a door, a tap, the click of the
switch, the kettle's rising note. The room's night ends the moment I walk into
it. I have started to feel slightly rude about this — barging in on a space that
was doing something. But it always seems glad. The radiator knocks twice.
Somebody polite, asking to be let in.
