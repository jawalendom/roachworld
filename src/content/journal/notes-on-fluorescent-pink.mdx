Fluorescent pink is not a colour so much as an argument. It does not sit on the
page; it hovers a little above it. Under the wrong light it looks cheap, and
under the right light it looks like the future arrived and it was slightly
embarrassing.

Risograph printers love it because the ink is genuinely fluorescent — it takes
in ultraviolet you cannot see and hands it back as visible light, so a flat
shape appears to be lit from inside. Photographs run through it stop being
records of things and become moods.

> Pick the loud ink. You can always print the quiet issue next time.

Roachworld is set in this pink and a medium blue, over black, on a paper the
colour of a paperback left in a window. The two inks overprint to make a bruised
purple that neither can make alone, and that overlap — slightly misaligned, a
little wrong — is the whole aesthetic. A magazine should look like it was made by
a hand that could have slipped.
