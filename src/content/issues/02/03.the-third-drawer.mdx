Every kitchen has one. Ours is third from the top, left of the sink. It does not
close all the way any more. This is a complete list of what is in it, in the
order my hand found it.

- **Three rubber bands, one perished.** The perished one snapped when I picked it
  up and left a grey mark on my thumb that took a day to wear off.
- **A single chopstick.** Pale wood, slightly warped. Its partner has never once
  turned up. I have kept it for four years on the theory that the day I throw it
  out is the day the other one appears.
- **A key that opens nothing in this apartment.** Brass, worn smooth. It came
  with the place, in this drawer, and I have added it back every time I have
  cleaned the drawer out, which means at this point I am its custodian.
- **Twine.** A useful amount. The one unambiguously good citizen of the drawer.
- **A takeout menu for a restaurant that closed in 2019.** The photo of the
  soup on the front has faded to a warm brown that is, I will admit, close to
  the house colour.
- **Batteries.** Provenance unknown, charge unknown. To test them would be to
  take a position on them, and I am not ready.
- **A fortune from a cookie**, folded twice: *You will find what you are looking
  for in the last place you look.* Which is either profound or a tautology, and
  either way it lives in the drawer now, which feels correct.

> A junk drawer is not a failure of organisation. It is the one place in the
> house you are allowed to defer a decision indefinitely.

I closed it as far as it goes. The chopstick is still in there. So is the key.
