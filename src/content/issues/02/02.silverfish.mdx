There is a silverfish that lives in the third grout line from the tub. I have
seen it maybe forty times over two years, always in the same eight inches of
seam, always at night, always moving like it has somewhere to be and no legs to
speak of.

I looked it up once. They eat starch — book glue, wallpaper paste, the sizing in
paper, the film of skin cells and soap that settles into a bathroom. They are
older than the dinosaurs by a hundred million years and they have not felt the
need to update the design. They can go a year without eating. They molt their
whole lives. They are, functionally, a small silver argument that most of what a
building does to keep you comfortable also keeps something else fed.

> It is not my bathroom. It is the bathroom. I am the one on a lease.

I am not going to pretend this is a friendship. We have no relationship. It does
not know I exist in any way that matters, and if it did it would not care. But I
have started leaving the light off when I get up at night, partly so I do not
have to watch it freeze mid-seam and pretend to be a crack in the caulk, and
partly because the dark is more honestly ours to share.
