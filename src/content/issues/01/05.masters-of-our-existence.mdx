## Amsterdam

A lot to say so bear with me

I won't say too much about the urban planning cause I've already talked about that many times about other cities and everyone knows Dutch urban planning is world class

The public transit, the bike lanes (I could even see them from the plane) are all incredibly well organised - couples riding together, children riding alone, parents riding with their small children in a trolley thing - designed to maximise quality of life and contentment

{/* read-more */}

But instead what I noticed is how intentional the infrastructure is designed. Even coming in from the plane I could see the canals, the dams (dijks), the farmland, all organised together almost like it was designed on a computer program like cities skylines or something - it almost felt artificial in how well it was organised (I mean it is artificial technically but you know what I mean)

Consider this - the Netherlands is one of the most low lying countries on earth and over the centuries they have built an intricate system of water engineering that not only prevents the flooding but has even reclaimed land for agriculture and development

> Basically what I'm getting at is the Netherlands is a country that is the master of its existence and not enslaved to it

In Africa unfortunately the reverse is true. Our lack of agency over our own existence leaves us and our lives at the whims of forces beyond our control with no way to meet the challenges. Aside from the pacific islands and the polar regions, Africa is the region that will be most affected by global warming and climate change. The effects this will have will ravage our societies and create famines, wars and potentially stunt advancement for centuries again because we have no sovereignty over our own existence. It was historically taken from us and is now willingly surrendered by our ruling classes

But I've been reflecting on the non-political 😉 ways of reclaiming this sovereign ownership over land for climate mitigation or adaptation. Unfortunately the UN basically said 1.5C is inevitable so i think it's time to accept climate change as unavoidable and start preparing for it. If African countries - especially the Sahel - could exhibit a similar mastery over their environments the way the Dutch have, it could be the foundation for its transformation

But if we fail to do this it's another century of stagnation

Sorry for political rant lol but aside from that, great vibes and culture (even though I didn't stay very long and lowkey fell asleep on the canal cause I didn't sleep for 2 days)

People seem very happy and quality of life seems very high, probably the highest of any city I've been to aside from Basel

So all in all W city and will be back

> 10/10
